Hospital EMR — User Manual

This manual describes Hospital EMR as it exists in the frozen hospital UAT release (v1.0.0-uat). It is for hospital staff and trainers.

It is not a product brochure. If something is not available in this release, this package says so.

Release: v1.0.0-uat · commit 2ea80c40c02cd42aa33fba47a0aa9dc1cc7d9ff6

What is Hospital EMR?

Hospital EMR is the hospital’s daily work system for Hospital EMR Hospital & Wellness Centre.

Staff sign in once and open their department. They register patients, see today’s visits, record consultations, order tests and medicines, nurse admitted patients, dispense paid prescriptions, collect payment, and review hospital activity.

Hospital EMR is the staff-facing workflow layer. It does not replace the hospital’s clinical and financial records. Those remain in the systems already used for the chart, laboratory processing, billing, and imaging.

What staff useWhat it is for
Hospital EMRDepartment screens, hospital language, daily steps, department pricing, cashier, ward board, MAR, analytics
Clinical chart enginePatient identity, visits, diagnoses, prescriptions, observations, admissions
Laboratory processingSpecimen collection, accession, testing, result entry, validation
Billing and stockInvoices, payments, receipts, pharmacy stock movements
Imaging archiveScan files, when a modality is connected. Images are not shown inside Hospital EMR in this release.

Staff should think in hospital tasks: register, consult, collect specimen, dispense, collect payment. They should not need to know the names of the engines behind the screens.

An account only opens the departments assigned to it. Signing in does not let a nurse collect payment or a doctor change laboratory prices.

How the hospital works in Hospital EMR

Clinical workflow

  1. Reception finds or registers the patient and starts a visit.
  2. The doctor opens that visit, records the consultation, and may diagnose, order tests or imaging, prescribe, or request admission.
  3. Laboratory, Pharmacy, and Radiology work from those orders.
  4. The doctor reviews results and reports on the same visit.
  5. If admitted, Nursing assigns a bed and records notes, vitals, and scheduled medicines.
  6. Nursing discharges the patient and frees the bed.
  7. The visit and records remain available for later care.

Payment is not required before the doctor can save a consultation or place an order. Unpaid work is visible. Collection happens at Accountant.

Financial workflow

  1. A department creates a charge (registration card, consultation fee, laboratory fee, imaging price, pharmacy prescription, accommodation).
  2. That charge becomes a bill the cashier can collect.
  3. Accountant records payment.
  4. The department screen then shows Paid (or pharmacy Ready to dispense).
  5. Completing the service is a separate step (dispense, process a specimen, write a report).

Paid does not mean the work is finished.

Changing today’s catalogue price does not change a bill already created.

New departmental bills use naira (₦). The financial company currency may still be US dollars. Older accommodation bills that were in dollars stay in dollars. See BILLING_AND_PAYMENTS.md.

Administrative workflow

Hospital Admin creates and disables staff accounts, assigns departments, and reviews hospital counts and staff-administration activity.

Hospital Admin does not process specimens, write clinical notes, or collect payment (unless that person also has those department rights).

System Settings is not available in this release.

How information moves (staff view)

Patient arrives
    → Reception: find or register, start visit, card fee
    → Clinical: consultation, orders, prescription, admit if needed
        → Laboratory: see order → process in laboratory processing → result returns
        → Pharmacy: wait for payment → dispense
        → Radiology: price and report (images not in Hospital EMR)
    → Doctor reviews results and reports
    → Nursing: bed, notes, vitals, scheduled medicines
    → Nursing: discharge
    → Accountant: collect any unpaid bills
    → Records remain on the patient

Only steps that this release supports are listed. Details are in CLINICAL_WORKFLOWS.md.

Signing in

  1. Open the hospital Hospital EMR address on a supported browser.
  2. Enter your hospital username and your own password.
  3. Select Sign in.
  4. Hospital EMR opens your department. You do not pick a room or ward as a workplace.

Wrong username or password: Could not sign in — *Incorrect username or password.*

If the network cannot be reached: Unable to connect — try again. If that continues, tell Hospital Admin.

Do not share accounts. Do not use another person’s password. Do not use development passwords on the hospital system.

Sign out when you leave the desk.

Finding a patient

Most departments use the header search:

Search by name, hospital number, or phone (type at least two characters).

Always confirm name and hospital number before you record, order, dispense, administer, or collect money.

Patient Account

Patient Account is a read-only money view: billed so far, already received, still unpaid, and department bills.

Reception, Clinical, Nursing, Laboratory, Pharmacy, Radiology, and Accountant can open it when they can already open that patient. Only Accountant collects payment.

Open full chart

Some department patient screens offer Open full chart. That opens the full clinical chart for extra detail.

Use it when you need the wider chart. Daily work stays in Hospital EMR. Do not treat extra chart screens as Hospital EMR department procedures.

Open full chart is not shown on the Doctor consultation screen in this release. It is available on Nursing, Laboratory, and Pharmacy patient screens.

What staff should never do

  • Share a username or password
  • Register a new patient without searching first
  • Record work on the wrong hospital number
  • Change times on vitals or medication administration (time is recorded automatically)
  • Bypass payment to dispense (the system blocks unpaid dispensing)
  • Enter or invent laboratory results in Hospital EMR (results are entered in laboratory processing)
  • Dispense a medicine from the ward MAR, or administer a dose from Pharmacy
  • Delete or “fix” records in hidden systems
  • Use development passwords on the hospital server

Known limitations (this release)

These are accepted UAT limits, not hidden defects.

  • A new laboratory result should be processed during hospital UAT. Do not rely only on an older result from another environment.
  • The laboratory board can take several seconds to load.
  • New departmental bills use naira. The company currency may still be US dollars. Older accommodation invoices in dollars stay in dollars.
  • Nursing Diagnoses may show as unavailable.
  • Some Nursing sections show Coming soon (tasks, care plan, intake and output).
  • SuperAdmin can still change department catalogue prices and accommodation tariffs.
  • Wards without a configured accommodation tariff show 0 until a price is set.
  • When-needed (PRN) medicines are not added to the scheduled medication sheet.
  • Radiology images are not shown in Hospital EMR.
  • System Settings is not currently available in this release.
  • Doctor Refer, Follow-up disposition, and doctor discharge decision are not currently available in this release. Nursing performs discharge from the ward.
  • Examination, Review of Systems, Family History, and Social History are not currently available on the Doctor consultation screen.
  • Cashier refunds are not currently available in this release.
  • Four laboratory tests remain unordered from Hospital EMR (Inorganic Phosphate, Testosterone, Progesterone, Estradiol) because they have no safe hospital catalogue mapping.

Reception — Department guide

This guide describes Reception as it works in this release. Payment is collected at Accountant, not at Reception.

Related: User manual · Billing · Department pricing

What Reception is for

Reception finds or registers the patient, starts the right kind of visit, bills the registration card, and sends the patient to Clinical, Emergency, or the ward as needed.

Screens: Dashboard · Search Patients · Register Patient · Today's Visits · Pricing · Patient Accounts (search)

Logging in

PURPOSE: Open the Reception desk.

WHO DOES IT: Staff with Reception or Registration access.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open Hospital EMR and enter your hospital username and password.
  2. Select Sign in.
  3. You open Reception. You do not pick a room.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Your session stays in Reception until you sign out or switch departments (only if your account has more than one).

COMMON ERRORS: *Could not sign in* — incorrect username or password.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Try again. If it still fails, contact Hospital Admin. Do not use someone else’s account.

SECURITY NOTES: Use only your own password. Sign out when you leave the desk.

Finding an existing patient

PURPOSE: Open the correct person. Avoid a second registration.

WHO DOES IT: Reception.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Use Search by name, hospital number, or phone (type at least two characters).
  2. Confirm name and hospital number.
  3. Open the patient.

Search Patients and Patient Accounts focus the same search.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: The patient record opens. No new hospital number is created.

COMMON ERRORS: Two people with similar names; phone not stored so phone search finds nothing.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Search again by hospital number. If you still cannot find them, do not register until you have checked with a supervisor.

SECURITY NOTES: Never open a record that is not this patient.

Registering a new patient

PURPOSE: Create a hospital record and assign a hospital number.

WHO DOES IT: Reception.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Search first.
  2. Open Register Patient.
  3. Choose Individual Card or Family Card. The fee is billed after the visit starts. Reception does not collect payment.
  4. Enter first name, last name, sex, date of birth or age, and contact and address as required.
  5. Watch Existing patients after you type three or more characters of the first and last name. Open an existing record if it is the same person.
  6. Select Save.

You do not type the hospital number. It is assigned when the patient is saved.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: A hospital number is assigned (for example an ABC number). You can start a visit and create the card fee.

COMMON ERRORS: *Similar patients found* — the system found possible matches.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Open the existing record unless you are sure this is a new person. Use Register anyway only when a supervisor agrees it is a different person.

SECURITY NOTES: Do not invent a hospital number. Do not register a duplicate to “make a new visit.”

Starting a visit

PURPOSE: Record why the patient is here today.

WHO DOES IT: Reception.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open the patient.
  2. Select Start visit.
  3. Choose one:
What you selectMeaning
General OutpatientRegular outpatient consultation
Follow-upReturning for previous treatment or condition
EmergencyUrgent care
AdmissionPatient is being admitted — select the ward shown on the screen
  1. Confirm.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: The visit is created. You may see Visit successfully created and a visit number. Today's Visits lists visits that started today.

Outpatient and follow-up visits started today appear on the doctor’s Today's Patients. Emergency and admission visits do not appear on that outpatient list.

COMMON ERRORS: *This patient already has an open visit.* Start another only if they are arriving for a different kind of care.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Check Today's Visits and the patient screen. Do not create extra visits to “reset” the list.

SECURITY NOTES: Do not start a visit on the wrong patient.

Registration card fee

PURPOSE: Send the Individual Card or Family Card bill to Accountant.

WHO DOES IT: Reception, after a visit exists.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. On the patient screen, open Registration card fee.
  2. Confirm Individual Card or Family Card.
  3. Select Create registration fee and confirm. Copy: *This sends the bill to Accountant. Reception does not collect payment.*

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Status shows Not billed yet, UNPAID, or PAID. When paid: *Payment received* — the receipt is on Patient Account.

One fee per visit. Submitting again reuses the existing charge.

COMMON ERRORS: Trying to collect cash in Reception.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Direct the patient to Accountant. Open Patient Account to see whether the bill exists.

SECURITY NOTES: Reception cannot collect payment. Do not record payment on paper as if Hospital EMR received it.

Card pricing

PURPOSE: Set the selling price of Individual Card and Family Card for new fees.

WHO DOES IT: Reception (read and write). Accountant and Hospital Admin can view. SuperAdmin can also change these prices.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open Pricing.
  2. Change the naira amount if required.
  3. Save. Add a reason when asked.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: New card fees use the new price. Price history stores the old price, new price, who changed it, and when.

COMMON ERRORS: Expecting an old unpaid or paid bill to change.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: That is correct behaviour. Yesterday’s bill keeps yesterday’s amount. See Department pricing.

SECURITY NOTES: Do not change prices unless you are authorised. Changing a price does not rewrite existing bills.

Directing the patient and Patient Account

After the visit starts, send the patient to Clinical (or Emergency / the ward as started).

Patient Account is read-only: billed so far, already received, still unpaid. Tell the patient to pay at Accountant.

  • There is no separate waiting-room queue. Today's Visits are visits that started today.
  • An unpaid card fee does not block the doctor from opening the chart.
  • Phone search only works when a phone number is stored.

Doctor — Department guide

This guide describes Clinical (Doctor) as it works in this release.

Related: User manual · Nursing · Laboratory · Pharmacy

What Clinical is for

The doctor sees today’s outpatients, records the consultation, diagnoses, orders laboratory and imaging tests, prescribes, and may request admission.

Screens: Dashboard · Today's Patients · My Patients · Patients (search) · Consultation

Dashboard and Today's Patients show the same Today's Patients list.

Logging in and finding patients

PURPOSE: Open today’s work.

WHO DOES IT: Staff with Clinical access.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Sign in with your hospital username. Clinical opens.
  2. Use Today's Patients for outpatient and follow-up visits that started today.
  3. Use My Patients for people you have already attended (after a saved consultation).
  4. Use header search for any patient.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Opening a name continues that visit’s consultation.

COMMON ERRORS: An emergency or admission visit does not appear on Today's Patients. That is by design.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Search by hospital number. Ask Reception whether the visit was started as General Outpatient or Follow-up.

SECURITY NOTES: Confirm name and hospital number before you write.

Consultation

PURPOSE: Record this visit’s clinical work.

WHO DOES IT: Doctor.

Consultation flow: Review → Vitals → Consultation → Previous visits → Diagnose → Order → Prescribe → Admission

Not currently available in this release on this screen:

  • Examination
  • Review of Systems
  • Past Medical History, Medication History, Family History, Social History
  • Refer / Follow-up as a disposition
  • Doctor discharge decision
  • Open full chart (available on Nursing, Laboratory, and Pharmacy patient screens)

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Confirm patient and visit.
  2. Record complaint, history, and notes.
  3. Record outpatient vitals if needed (open OPD visit required).
  4. Record diagnosis.
  5. Order laboratory or imaging tests if needed.
  6. Prescribe if needed.
  7. Select Save consultation.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Notes, vitals (OPD), diagnoses, and prescriptions for this visit are stored. Payment is not required to save.

COMMON ERRORS: *Visit has not started* — Reception has not started a visit. *Medications unavailable* / search unavailable — try again; if it persists, contact Hospital Admin.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Do not start a second visit yourself unless you have Reception access. Ask Reception to start the correct visit.

SECURITY NOTES: Do not save on the wrong visit. Previous-visit encounter lines do not show note text.

Laboratory orders and results

PURPOSE: Order hospital laboratory tests and later review results.

WHO DOES IT: Doctor orders. Laboratory processes. Accountant collects the fee.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. In Orders, search the hospital laboratory list.
  2. Add tests and submit. A laboratory fee is sent to Accounts.
  3. Tell the patient that fees are paid at Accounts.
  4. When Laboratory has released a result, it appears under Laboratory results.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: The order exists for Laboratory. Processing happens in laboratory processing, not on this screen. Results return here when released.

COMMON ERRORS: Result still empty — the specimen may not have been processed yet. *Laboratory orders unavailable* / *Laboratory results unavailable* — reload; if it continues, tell Hospital Admin.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Check Laboratory. Do not invent a result. Do not enter results in Hospital EMR.

SECURITY NOTES: Payment is not required to place the order. Paid does not mean the result is ready.

Radiology orders and reports

PURPOSE: Request an approved hospital examination and later read the report.

WHO DOES IT: Doctor orders. Radiology sets price and writes the report.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. In Orders, choose the hospital ultrasound or radiography name.
  2. Submit. Imaging fees are paid at Accounts.
  3. Read Radiology reports when Radiology has saved a report.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Radiology sees the examination. Images are not shown in Hospital EMR in this release.

COMMON ERRORS: *Radiology reports unavailable* or *No report recorded for this examination.*

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Ask Radiology. Do not treat a missing image viewer as a failed order.

SECURITY NOTES: You do not collect imaging payment.

Prescriptions

PURPOSE: Prescribe medication for this visit.

Lifecycle in this release:

Doctor prescribes
    → prescription / drug order
    → bill created for Accounts
    → Accountant collects payment
    → Pharmacy sees Ready to dispense
    → Pharmacy dispenses
    → patient receives the medicine

If the patient is later admitted, scheduled inpatient doses appear on Nursing MAR. That is administration, not dispensing. Pharmacy still dispenses the supply.

When-needed (PRN) medicines are not added to the scheduled medication sheet.

COMMON ERRORS: Expecting Pharmacy to dispense before payment. The system blocks unpaid dispensing.

Admission

PURPOSE: Ask Nursing to admit the patient.

WHO DOES IT: Doctor.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open Admission.
  2. Select Admit Patient. Copy: *The doctor decides to admit. Nursing assigns the bed. This does not collect payment.*

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: *Admission requested. Nursing will assign a bed.* You do not choose the bed.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Check Nursing Admissions. Do not invent a bed assignment.

After admission, on consultation you can read nursing notes, inpatient vitals, and MAR. You cannot record them.

Consultation fee

On an active visit, the doctor can Create consultation fee in naira. Status UNPAID / PAID. Copy: *Please proceed to the cashier.*

You cannot collect payment. An unpaid fee does not block saving the consultation.

Inpatient visibility

When the patient is admitted (or after discharge, for history):

  • Inpatient vitals — read only
  • Nursing notes — read only
  • MAR — read only

Nursing notes after discharge remain readable to the doctor even when Nursing can no longer add a note.

What the doctor cannot do

  • Collect payment
  • Assign, transfer, or discharge a bed (Nursing)
  • Record inpatient vitals, nursing notes, or MAR
  • Enter laboratory results
  • Dispense medicines
  • Change department catalogue prices
  • Open Hospital Administration analytics

Nursing — Department guide

This guide describes Nursing as it works in this release. It is a safety-critical document. Do not bypass identification, times, or administration windows.

Related: Inpatient / ward · Doctor · Pharmacy

What Nursing is for

Nursing runs the ward: who is on a bed, who is waiting for a bed, notes, vitals, and scheduled medication administration.

Screens: Dashboard · Ward Board · Patients · Admissions · Transfers · Discharges · inpatient chart

Pharmacy dispenses medicine. Nursing administers scheduled inpatient doses. These are different jobs.

Logging in

PURPOSE: Open the ward.

WHO DOES IT: Staff with Nursing access.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Sign in with your hospital username.
  2. Nursing opens. You do not pick a ward as a workplace.

SECURITY NOTES: Use only your account. Your name is recorded on notes, vitals, and administrations.

Ward Board, Patients, Admissions

PURPOSE: See occupancy and open the right chart.

WHO DOES IT: Nursing.

Dashboard shows occupied beds, available beds, and patients waiting for a bed, plus ward overview counts.

Ward Board shows Ward → Room → Bed → Patient. Beds are Available or Occupied. Occupied beds open the chart and offer Transfer and Discharge. Available beds open Assign Bed.

Patients lists occupied patients and those with Admit requested.

Admissions lists patients the doctor has asked to admit. Nursing assigns the bed.

Transfers and Discharges are shortcuts for people currently on a bed. They are not a history list.

STEP-BY-STEP (open a chart)

  1. Confirm name and hospital number.
  2. Open the patient from the board or Patients.
  3. Check Ward, Bed, and status (Occupied, Admit requested, or Not admitted).

SECURITY NOTES: Always match the person at the bedside to the hospital number on the screen.

Assign bed, transfer, discharge

PURPOSE: Place the patient, move the patient, or free the bed.

WHO DOES IT: Nursing (bed assignment rights).

Assign bed

  1. Open Admissions, an available bed, or the patient chart.
  2. Confirm Admit requested when the doctor has asked to admit.
  3. Choose the ward and bed shown.
  4. Select Assign Bed.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: The bed is occupied. You may see *Admitted to …* Accommodation billing may start. Nursing does not collect payment. Unpaid accommodation does not block assign.

If the bed was just taken: *This bed has just been assigned to another patient. Please choose another bed.*

You can assign from the chart in an emergency even if the doctor has not requested admit. Use that only when the hospital requires it.

Transfer

  1. From the occupied bed or Transfers, select Transfer.
  2. Choose the new ward and bed.
  3. Select Transfer Patient.

There is no separate transfer history screen.

Discharge

  1. Confirm identity.
  2. Select Discharge. Confirm: *Discharge this patient and release their bed?*

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: *Patient discharged. Bed released.* Historical notes, vitals, and MAR remain readable where the system allows. You cannot record new notes, vitals, or administrations after discharge.

Unpaid accommodation does not block discharge. Send the family to Accountant for any unpaid bills.

WHAT NOT TO DO: Do not discharge the wrong patient. Do not try to “delete” the stay.

Inpatient chart

Header shows name, hospital number, age, ward, bed, and allergy line.

Sections that work in this release: alerts, current condition, Vitals, Medication Administration (MAR), Inpatient Nursing Notes, doctor instructions (read-only), admission and discharge actions.

Coming soon — not currently available in this release:

  • Nursing tasks
  • Care plan
  • Intake and output

Diagnoses may show Diagnoses unavailable. That is a known limit. Do not invent a diagnosis.

Open full chart opens the wider clinical chart. Daily ward work stays in Hospital EMR.

Patient Account is read-only.

Nursing Notes

PURPOSE: Record an observation during this admission.

WHO DOES IT: Nursing, while the patient has an active bed and admission.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open the inpatient chart.
  2. Confirm identity.
  3. Select + Nursing Note.
  4. Write the observation, care, and response.
  5. Select Save Nursing Note.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: The note is stored with your name and the current date and time, recorded automatically. You cannot edit or delete it. A later note does not replace an earlier one.

WHO CAN READ: Nursing during the active admission. The doctor can read current and historical notes.

After discharge, Nursing may see Nursing notes unavailable on the chart. The doctor can still read historical notes.

COMMON ERRORS: Saving a note on the wrong patient. Trying to change an old note.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Write a new note. Do not ask anyone to change the time.

SECURITY NOTES: Time and author cannot be typed by you. Do not share an account so that another nurse’s name is recorded as yours.

Vitals

PURPOSE: Record inpatient observations.

WHO DOES IT: Nursing during an active admission. Doctor reviews only.

STEP-BY-STEP

  1. Open the inpatient chart → Vitals.
  2. Confirm identity.
  3. Enter the measurements you took.
  4. Review warnings for unusual values. Unusual values can still be saved when they are true.
  5. Confirm. Copy: observations are added and do not replace earlier readings.

BMI may display from weight and height. It is calculated on screen and is not saved as a stored observation.

Both systolic and diastolic must be entered together. Systolic must be higher than diastolic. Values outside the allowed range are refused.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER SAVING: Time is recorded automatically. Each recording is a new observation. History is kept. Later measurements do not overwrite earlier ones.

After discharge you can read history. You cannot record new vitals (*Vitals can be recorded during an active admission*).

OPD versus ward: The doctor may record outpatient vitals on an OPD visit. Those are not the same as ward vitals. The condition summary may show a latest reading from a wider history; the Vitals section shows this admission’s recordings.

WHAT NOT TO DO: Do not change timestamps. Do not record another nurse’s work under your name.

Medication administration (MAR)

PURPOSE: Give or document a scheduled inpatient dose.

WHO DOES IT: Nursing during an active admission.

Doctor prescribes (inpatient)
    → medication schedule is created for eligible orders
    → scheduled dose appears on MAR
    → Nurse administers (or records not given)
    → administration is stored

When-needed (PRN) medicines are not added to the scheduled medication sheet. They cannot be administered from MAR in this release.

Outpatient-only orders and incomplete prescriptions also do not become scheduled slots.

Window

Normal window: 30 minutes before the scheduled time through 60 minutes after.

  • Inside the window: Administer for Given (note optional).
  • Outside the window: use Administer outside scheduled window. A reason is required, including when the dose was given.
  • Not done, Refused, and Held always need a reason.

Actual time is recorded automatically. You cannot type or change it. Scheduled time and actual time are stored separately.

Outcomes

You selectMeaning
GivenDose administered
Not doneDose not given
RefusedPatient refused
HeldHeld

A finished slot cannot be administered again. After discharge the sheet is historical — readable, no new administrations.

Duplicate protection: Repeating the same save does not create a second administration.

Patient isolation: A slot for another patient is refused (*This scheduled dose is for another patient*).

COMMON ERRORS: *This dose is outside the normal administration window.* *Medication administration unavailable.* Empty MAR because the order is PRN or not inpatient.

WHAT TO DO IF SOMETHING FAILS: Confirm you are on the correct patient and admission. Use the outside-window action with a true clinical reason when the time is outside the window. Do not invent a scheduled time. Do not administer from Pharmacy.

SECURITY NOTES: Do not bypass the window. Do not administer after discharge. Do not record a dose you did not give.

  • Change accommodation tariffs or department catalogue prices
  • Dispense from Pharmacy
  • Write diagnoses or prescriptions
  • Request admission (doctor) or process laboratory specimens
  • Edit or delete saved notes, vitals, or MAR rows
  • Administer PRN as scheduled MAR
  • Backdate times

  • Tasks, care plan, and intake/output show Coming soon

Troubleshooting

For hospital staff. Do not change hidden records or share accounts.

I cannot log in

Possible cause: Wrong username or password; account disabled; network.

Check: Caps lock. Your own username. Hospital address.

Do: Try again. Ask Hospital Admin whether the account is disabled.

Contact Hospital Admin: After two or three failures, or if many staff fail at once.

Do not: Use another person’s account or a development password.

The EMR is unavailable / Unable to connect

Possible cause: Network, power, or the hospital system is down.

Check: Another computer on the hospital network. Whether others are affected.

Do: Wait and try again. If everyone is down, contact Hospital Admin.

Do not: Recreate patients on paper without a supervisor’s downtime plan.

I cannot find the patient

Possible cause: Spelling; they were never registered; you searched phone but no phone is stored.

Check: Hospital number. Alternate spelling. Ask Reception.

Do: Search again. Register only after a supervisor agrees this is a new person.

Do not: Create a duplicate record to “get on with it.”

The patient is not appearing in my department

Possible cause: Wrong visit type; not admitted; not ordered; not paid (Pharmacy).

Check:

  • Doctor Today's Patients: visit must be General Outpatient or Follow-up started today. Emergency and admission are excluded.
  • Nursing: doctor admit and/or bed assignment.
  • Laboratory: doctor submitted the order.
  • Pharmacy Ready to dispense: bill must be fully paid.
  • Radiology: an imaging order exists.

Do: Ask the department that starts that step.

Do not: Start extra visits or extra registrations to force the list.

The medication is not appearing on MAR

Possible cause: When-needed (PRN) medicine; outpatient order; not admitted; schedule not created yet.

Check: The patient is occupied on a bed. The prescription is an inpatient scheduled order, not PRN.

Do: PRN is not on the scheduled sheet in this release. Use the doctor and hospital policy outside MAR. Reload the chart.

Do not: Administer from Pharmacy as if it were MAR.

I cannot administer the medication

Possible cause: Outside the window; already recorded; discharged; wrong patient.

Check: Scheduled versus actual window (30 minutes early / 60 minutes late). Slot status. Admission still active.

Do: Use Administer outside scheduled window with a true reason when the time is outside the window. Confirm identity.

Do not: Change the clock or ask anyone to backdate.

The medication is outside the administration window

Possible cause: You are more than 30 minutes early or more than 60 minutes late.

Do: Use the outside-window action and record the clinical reason. Time is stored automatically.

Do not: Skip the reason or record a false time.

The laboratory order is not visible

Possible cause: Order not submitted; different patient; board still loading.

Check: Doctor consultation orders. Hospital number. Wait several seconds and refresh — the board can be slow.

Do: Ask the doctor to confirm submit.

Do not: Create a new patient.

The laboratory result is not visible

Possible cause: Specimen not collected; result not validated; processing not finished.

Check: Laboratory processing, not only Hospital EMR.

Do: Complete collection, testing, and validation in laboratory processing. Process a new result during UAT.

Do not: Type a result into Hospital EMR or invent a value.

The prescription is not available for dispensing

Possible cause: Unpaid or partial payment; already dispensed; no stock.

Check: Accountant. Stock on the prescription.

Do: Send the patient to pay. Do not dispense unpaid medication.

Do not: Bypass the block.

The patient has not paid

Possible cause: Charge not created; cashier not collected.

Check: Patient Account. Which department should have created the fee.

Do: Reception / Doctor / Radiology / Pharmacy path as appropriate, then Accountant.

Do not: Treat unpaid as dispensed or as a completed laboratory result.

The accommodation charge looks wrong

Possible cause: Tariff was 0; old bill in dollars; price changed after the bill.

Check: Whether the bill is old. Whether that ward has a tariff.

Do: Collect the billed amount. Ask SuperAdmin to set missing tariffs for new days. Do not expect an old bill to change.

Do not: Edit hidden financial records.

The price I changed did not change an old bill

Possible cause: This is how the system works.

Do: Explain that the bill keeps the price from the day it was created.

Do not: Create a fake second bill to “fix” history unless a supervisor starts a formal correction process.

The EMR is slow

Possible cause: Laboratory board load; busy hour; workstation.

Check: Whether only Laboratory is slow (known). Whether all screens are slow.

Do: Wait for the laboratory board. Try again. Tell Hospital Admin if the whole system is unusable.

Do not: Click submit repeatedly on payments or dispense.

Diagnoses unavailable / Coming soon on Nursing

Possible cause: Known limits of this release.

Do: Continue notes, vitals, and MAR. Ask the doctor for the diagnosis.

Do not: Invent a diagnosis or use Coming soon boxes as if they stored data.

  • The system is down
  • Repeated errors after you confirmed identity and payment

System architecture

This document is for Hospital Admin, implementers, and technical support. Staff desk manuals avoid these names.

Release: v1.0.0-uat.

Related: Hospital deployment · Deployment secrets

Non-technical: how information moves

  1. A staff member signs in to Hospital EMR in the browser.
  2. Hospital EMR shows the department that person is allowed to use.
  3. When they save clinical work (consultation, diagnosis, prescription, visit), it is stored in the clinical chart.
  4. When they create a charge or collect payment, the financial record is stored in the billing system.
  5. Laboratory processing (specimen, accession, result entry) happens in the laboratory system. Hospital EMR only shows orders and released results.
  6. Imaging files, if a scanner is connected, live in the imaging archive. Hospital EMR does not show images in this release.
  7. Ward notes, vitals, and MAR are recorded through Hospital EMR and stored as clinical observations / administrations on the admission.

Hospital EMR does not keep a second patient database. It does not invent a second invoice book.

Technical: request path

    → Bahmni proxy (ports 80 / 443)
        → Hospital EMR UI (`/` and `/abikan/` from `abikan-shell/dist`)
        → Hospital EMR API (`/openmrs/abikan-api` → abikan-api)
        → OpenMRS / Bahmni clinical apps
        → OpenELIS (`/openelis`)
        → other Bahmni paths (documents, IPD UI, and so on)

Hospital EMR API
    → OpenMRS REST (and MySQL for admin analytics)
    → OpenELIS (server-side read of accession status only)
    → Odoo XML-RPC (charges, invoices, payments, stock)

Why there is no duplicate clinical database

Hospital EMR reads and writes the existing engines. A nursing note is an observation on the admission encounter. A prescription is an OpenMRS drug order; the bill is an Odoo invoice. A laboratory result displayed in Hospital EMR is the result already posted to OpenMRS after OpenELIS validation.

Observations and vitals

  • OPD vitals: doctor saves with the consultation encounter on an open OPD visit.
  • Inpatient vitals: nurse posts through Hospital EMR; each save creates new OpenMRS observations on the admission encounter. Time and author are server-owned. No overwrite, no client timestamp.

MAR scheduling

Bahmni IPD does not always create slots when the doctor saves a drug order. On MAR load, Hospital EMR asks the installed IPD schedule service to create schedules for eligible inpatient orders. PRN / as-needed orders are refused for the scheduled sheet. Administration uses the medication administration module; actual time is server clock.

Pharmacy

Atomfeed creates the commercial draft. Confirm / automated invoicing posts the invoice. Accountant payment is required. Pharmacist dispense validates the Odoo picking. Hospital EMR does not write an OpenMRS “Dispensed” observation in this release.

Billing

See BILLING_AND_PAYMENTS.md. Catalogue list_price is copied onto the charge line at creation. Historical account.move lines are not rewritten.

Company currency may remain USD while departmental pricelists and new charges use NGN.

Docker services (summary)

Bahmni Standard compose plus Hospital EMR overlay (docker-compose.override.yml). Hospital LAN hardening is docker-compose.hospital.yml (do not apply on the development Mac).

Hospital UI updates: git checkout v1.0.0-uat then npm ci && npm run build. Dist is generated, not tracked.

Never run docker compose down -v on a hospital host.
